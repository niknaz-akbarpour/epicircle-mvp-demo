The problem

Families are asked to coordinate too much across disconnected tools.

During and after a seizure, timing, communication, medication information and recovery checks can sit in different places. EpiCircle is designed around the whole care pathway—not a single alert.

⌁

Fragmented signals

Wearables, observations and seizure diaries may not form one understandable event record.

♡

Uncertain response

Families need to know who was alerted, who acknowledged and when escalation remains unresolved.

✓

Incomplete follow-through

Medication routines, recovery checks and information for later clinical review are often separate.

The proposed system

A closed-loop pathway built around five connected jobs.

Each layer has a defined role. AI supports pattern recognition, while people remain responsible for confirmation, care and clinical decisions.

1SenseReview motion, autonomic signals and device quality together.
2ConfirmGive the user a visible chance to cancel or confirm concern.
3AlertEscalate through a care circle and show receipt status.
4SupportKeep timing, location and the clinician-approved plan together.
5LearnCreate a structured record for later review and model improvement.
Product capabilities

Designed for the event, the response and the days between events.

AI

Explainable event evidence

Shows which signal groups contributed to a suspected ongoing convulsive pattern and whether signal quality was sufficient.

00

Event timer and action plan

Makes duration visible and keeps the individualized, clinician-approved action plan within reach.

♡

Care Circle escalation

Supports up to four prioritized caregivers with acknowledgement and escalation status.

⌖

Consent-based location

Requests location only with explicit consent and distinguishes real GPS from presentation-only sample points.

Rx

Medication routine support

Lets the patient self-confirm a scheduled dose and record concerns without recommending dose changes.

✓

Recovery and reassurance

Keeps acknowledgement, breathing, responsiveness, injury and return-to-baseline checks visible until completed.

Important: medication changes remain a clinician decision. The product concept never tells a patient to increase, reduce or stop a prescribed dose.

Safety, trust and boundaries

The interface should make uncertainty visible—not hide it.

Safety principles in the concept

✓Human confirmation before escalation whenever appropriate
✓Signal-quality and wear-status checks
✓Visible acknowledgement and unresolved-state tracking
✓Explicit consent before requesting location
✓Audit timestamps and false-alert feedback

What is not claimed

!It does not diagnose epilepsy.
!It does not predict all seizures or detect every seizure type.
!It does not replace emergency services or clinical judgment.
!It does not change medication doses.
!Current messaging, calling and AI detection remain simulated.
Development pathway

From an interactive MVP to a validated product.

This roadmap separates presentation-ready experience design from the evidence and infrastructure required for a real medical product.

PHASE 1 · NOWConcept and usability MVPTest workflows, language, accessibility and family needs with fictional data.
PHASE 2Data and algorithm studyDefine target event, collect consented multimodal data and establish annotation protocols.
PHASE 3Prospective validationEvaluate sensitivity, false alarms, subgroup performance and real-world usability.
PHASE 4Secure product and regulatory pathBuild integrations, privacy controls, quality systems and jurisdiction-specific regulatory evidence.

Privacy notice

This public website does not intentionally collect form submissions, patient records or health information. The on-page demo uses fictional data and runs in the browser.

The separately linked research MVP may store demonstration entries in the visitor’s browser. Do not enter real patient, emergency or identifying information. The hosting provider may process limited technical data under its own privacy terms.

Terms of responsible use

EpiCircle is a research-stage educational concept, not a medical device, diagnostic service or emergency-monitoring system. Content is not medical advice and no guarantee is made that a future product will detect or predict seizures.

Use local emergency services and a clinician-approved seizure action plan for real emergencies. Medication decisions must remain with a qualified prescribing clinician.
